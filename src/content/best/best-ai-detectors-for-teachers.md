## Common Questions

### 1. Is GPTZero more accurate than Turnitin for teachers?
Both platforms display strong performance within their intended environments based on public benchmarks. Turnitin is highly proficient at capturing machine text within standard student essays because its custom classifiers are trained explicitly on extensive datasets of human student writing variations. GPTZero is often preferred for qualitative classroom forensics because its interface visualizes perplexity and burstiness changes sentence-by-sentence. This gives educators a clear window into document construction while providing excellent protection against false positives on non-native English prose.

### 2. Can students buy private Turnitin accounts to test their essays?
Generally, no. Turnitin restricts individual direct consumer access and storefront purchasing to prevent automated text optimization loops. Access is governed exclusively by enterprise site licensing contracts managed directly by verified educational institutions and school boards. Students can only view their originality profiles if their specific course instructor enables that visibility setting within the institutional LMS assignment workspace.

### 3. Is it safe to use unauthorized online "Turnitin brokers"?
No, utilizing third-party online brokers that offer to run individual papers through Turnitin via shared or compromised institutional keys is highly unsafe. These unauthorized platforms frequently scrape submitted text, selling the files to essay mills or indexing them into unverified databases. When the official school instructor runs the final scan inside the university LMS, the student's document will trigger a high Plagiarism match against their own scraped draft, resulting in automated failure and disciplinary reviews.

### 4. Do AI detectors generate false positives on student writing?
Yes. AI detectors run on mathematical pattern probability calculations rather than absolute digital watermarks, meaning they can mistake unique human writing rhythms for machine text. Highly formal essays, technical descriptions, and prose written by non-native English speakers who naturally lean on standard transition phrases frequently trigger false positives on sensitive commercial-grade classifiers. Instructors should treat scores as general indicators rather than absolute proof of misconduct.

### 5. How do perplexity and burstiness impact classroom AI detection?
Perplexity measures the mathematical predictability of word choices within a text string, while burstiness tracks the structural variance in sentence length and formatting across a complete document. Human writers naturally construct text with structural chaos—mixing brief, punchy phrases with complex clauses and using unexpected synonyms. Generative AI models produce text with uniform sentence lengths and highly predictable word paths (low perplexity and low burstiness), allowing forensic engines to isolate machine signatures.

### 6. Can AI detection tools identify content processed through "humanizers"?
Professional-grade forensic engines continuously update their machine learning classifiers to identify the structural signatures left behind by automated text humanizers and rewriters. While basic pattern checkers can be bypassed, advanced semantic processing software evaluates document progression holistically, tracking machine frameworks even when vocabulary has been surface-scrubbed.

### 7. Does Turnitin track contract cheating or ghostwriting?
Yes, Turnitin offers a specialized administrative module known as Authorship forensics engineered specifically to track contract cheating. The system evaluates a student's historical metadata, tracking structural typing patterns, vocabulary complexity levels, and document properties across multiple semesters. If a new submittal departs radically from the student's established writing style profile, the software alerts administrators to a potential ghostwriting intervention.

### 8. Are student essays safe from commercial model training when scanned?
When utilizing enterprise-grade, education-first utilities like Turnitin, GPTZero (on paid tiers), or Copyleaks, student data privacy is strictly protected in alignment with FERPA and GDPR regulations. The platforms process documents via encrypted data channels and allow administrators to block files from being committed to public memory logs, ensuring student intellectual property is completely shielded from commercial AI training pools.

### 9. Should teachers rely only on AI detectors when grading?
No, AI detection should support—not replace—professional academic judgment. Because AI detectors calculate statistical probabilities rather than identifying hardcoded watermarks, they are prone to occasional false positives, especially when evaluating highly formal or non-native English writing. An elevated AI score should serve as an entry point for an educational dialogue rather than automated proof of cheating. Instructors should review historical student drafts and conduct quick oral follow-ups if structural anomalies appear to verify a student's understanding of the material.
